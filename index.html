Why do chemists use the mole?

Counting rice grains in a bucket is difficult. Atoms and molecules are unimaginably smaller! Chemists therefore weigh substances and use the mole as a counting unit.

9.1 · Relative atomic mass

⚖️ Relative Atomic Mass (Ar)

Atoms are too light to compare using everyday masses. Their masses are compared with carbon-12, which is assigned a value of exactly 12.

Ar = mass of one atom ÷ (1/12 × mass of one carbon-12 atom)
Reference standard
Remember
Ar is a ratio, so it has no unit.

Examples from the notes:

H = 1 · C = 12 · O = 16 · Cl = 35.5

Why is chlorine Ar = 35.5?

Chlorine has isotopes. Its relative atomic mass is a weighted average.

Ar = (75/100 × 35) + (25/100 × 37) = 35.5

🧮 Relative Molecular / Formula Mass (Mr)

Mr = sum of the Ar values of all atoms in the formula

Bracket rule
In Ca(NO3)2, the 2 outside multiplies everything inside: 2 N and 6 O.
Watch out
Covalent substances have relative molecular mass; ionic compounds have relative formula mass. Both use Mr. In CuSO4·5H2O, add 5 × 18 for the water.
9.3 · Count particles

🔢 The Mole & Avogadro Constant

Definition: One mole contains the same number of particles as there are atoms in 12 g of carbon-12. This number is 6 × 1023, the Avogadro constant. Unit: mol.
Moles = number of particles ÷ (6 × 1023)
Particles = moles × (6 × 1023)
Example 1

1 × 1023 neon atoms

÷ 6 × 1023

0.167 mol

Example 2

0.5 mol iron atoms

× 6 × 1023

3 × 1023

Example 3

3 mol H2 molecules

× 2 atoms per molecule

6 mol H atoms

Quick check: 0.5 mol O2 molecules and 0.5 mol Fe atoms contain the same number of particles. But identify what the particles are: atoms, molecules or ions.

9.4 · Mass ↔ moles

⚖️ Moles and Molar Mass

Molar mass is the mass of one mole. It has the same numerical value as Ar or Mr, expressed in g/mol.

Use the triangle to choose the operation
Moles = mass (g) ÷ Ar or Mr
Mass (g) = moles × Mr
Convert kg to g before using the formula. Example: 0.196 kg = 196 g; 196 ÷ 56 = 3.5 mol Fe.

9.5 · Percentage composition

🥧 Percentage by Mass

% of element = (Ar × number of atoms) ÷ Mr × 100%
Hydrogen peroxide

For H2O2 (Mr = 34):

H: (2 ÷ 34) × 100 = 5.9%

O: (32 ÷ 34) × 100 = 94.1%

Check: percentages of all elements should add to 100% (allowing for rounding).

Water in CuSO4·5H2O: (5 × 18) ÷ 250 × 100 = 36%.

Worked example: magnesium oxide
Mg = 1.20 g; O = 0.80 g. Moles: Mg = 1.20 ÷ 24 = 0.05; O = 0.80 ÷ 16 = 0.05. Ratio 1 : 1 → MgO.
Exam reminders
  • Use Ar, not Mr, when finding an empirical formula.
  • Mass of oxygen in an oxide can be found by subtraction: mass of oxide − mass of metal.
  • Do not round 2.6 to 3. Use a multiplier to reach whole numbers.

Empirical formula → molecular formula

n = Mr given ÷ Mr of empirical formula

Example: CH2O has Mr = 30. If the compound Mr is 180, n = 180 ÷ 30 = 6, so molecular formula = C6H12O6.

Fraction multipliers: 0.5 → ×2 · 0.33 or 0.67 → ×3 · 0.25 or 0.75 → ×4 · 0.2 → ×5.
9.7 · Gases at r.t.p.

🎈 Molar Gas Volume

Avogadro’s Law: Equal volumes of all gases at the same temperature and pressure contain the same number of molecules.
One mole fills 24 dm³ at r.t.p.
1 mol gas = 24 dm³ = 24 000 cm³
Moles = volume (dm³) ÷ 24
Moles = volume (cm³) ÷ 24 000
Volume (dm³) = moles × 24

Gas volume converter

6000 cm³ ÷ 24000 = 0.25 mol

Example: 8 g O2. Mr = 32; moles = 8 ÷ 32 = 0.25 mol; volume = 0.25 × 24 = 6 dm³.

Unit alert: Use 24 dm³ with dm³, or 24 000 cm³ with cm³. Never mix the units.
